\section{Theoretical Foundations \& Runtime Necessity Model}

\subsection{Decision-Preserving Sufficiency (ST-015)}
A representation $R \in \mathcal{R}$ is \emph{decision-sufficient} with respect to
optimal policy $\pi^*$ if:
\[
\pi^*(R) = \pi^*(S) \quad \forall S \in \mathcal{S}\text{ s.t. }R = \rho(S)
\]

\subsection{Runtime Necessity Formalization (ST-016)}
We formalize a TAKT Governed Runtime as a tuple $M = (\mathcal{C}, \pi_M)$, where
$\mathcal{C}$ is the runtime's capability set and $\pi_M : \mathcal{R} \to \mathcal{D}$
is its decision policy. The \emph{minimal governance kernel} is defined as:
\[
\mathcal{K}_D = \{C_{\text{contract}},\; C_{\text{uncertainty}},\; C_{\text{temporal}}\}
\]
A runtime $M$ is \emph{minimally governed} when $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{K}_D$, i.e.,
it contains exactly the capabilities proven necessary and no others.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Three definitions of decision preservation} appear across this
work at different layers of abstraction:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ST-015 (informational):} $\pi^*(R) = \pi^*(S)$ — the optimal policy
    yields the same decision on the compressed representation as on the full state.
    \item \textbf{Runtime alignment:} $\text{Sufficient}(M, \pi^*) \equiv \forall R,\; \pi_M(R) = \pi^*(R)$ — the runtime's policy matches the optimal policy on all representations.
    \item \textbf{Cross-runtime:} $\text{PreservesDecision}(M_1, M_2) \equiv \forall R,\; \pi_{M_1}(R) = \pi_{M_2}(R)$ — two runtime instances agree on all decisions.
\end{itemize}
These are related: $\text{Sufficient}(M, \pi^*)$ implies $\text{PreservesDecision}(M, M')$ for any $M'$ also sufficient w.r.t.\ $\pi^*$.

\textbf{Definition (Capability Necessity):} A capability $C \in \mathcal{C}$ is
\emph{locally necessary} for runtime $M$ if removing $C$ alters policy decisions
on at least one representation:
\[
\exists\, R \in \mathcal{R}:\; \pi_M(R) \;\neq\; \pi_{M \setminus \{C\}}(R)
\]

\textbf{Note on Axiom0:} The structural sufficiency module (ST-015) postulates
$\text{kernel}_D(x,y) \leftrightarrow K_D(x,y)$, asserting that the decision
kernel coincides with the intersection of capability kernels. This is a modelling
assumption, not a derived theorem, and is listed explicitly in the Non-Claims
of Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\textbf{Example 1 (Temporal Prefix Necessity):} Consider a runtime where
$C_{\text{temporal}}$ is ablated. Two trajectory-induced representations $R^1$
and $R^2$ share an identical terminal observation yet arise from trajectories
$\tau_1 = (r_0, r_1, r_2)$ (safe) and $\tau_2 = (r'_0, r'_1, r_2)$ (unsafe).
Here $\pi^*$ is defined over representations that encode trajectory history;
$R^1 \neq R^2$ because their history components differ.
A runtime inspecting only the terminal state $r_2$ produces:
$\pi_{M\setminus\{C_{\text{temporal}}}\}(R^1) = \pi_{M\setminus\{C_{\text{temporal}}}\}(R^2)$,
while the optimal policy correctly distinguishes them:
$\pi^*(R^1) \neq \pi^*(R^2)$, violating decision preservation.
