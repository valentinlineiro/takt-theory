\section{Empirical Validation \& Component Ablation (EXP-004)}

\subsection{Experimental Protocol}

EXP-004~\cite{takt_st016_2026} ablates one capability at a time from the reference
TypeScript runtime and records whether the runtime's decision diverges from the
full-capability baseline on a designated trigger scenario. Each scenario is
designed so that the ablated capability is the deciding factor.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{EXP-004 Ablation Protocol and Results}
\label{tab:exp004}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{p{2.2cm}p{2.6cm}p{1.5cm}p{1.5cm}p{1.2cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Ablation} & \textbf{Trigger scenario} & \textbf{Full policy} & \textbf{Ablated policy} & \textbf{Result} \\
\midrule
$C_{\text{contract}}$    & Contract-violating state    & STOP      & EXECUTE  & Diverge \\
$C_{\text{uncertainty}}$ & Margin boundary ($M_D\!\approx\!0$) & REFINE & EXECUTE & Diverge \\
$C_{\text{temporal}}$    & History-dependent prefix    & INTERVENE & MONITOR  & Diverge \\
\midrule
None (baseline)          & All trigger scenarios        & Correct   & N/A      & Stable  \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Each ablation divergence is manually encoded into a \texttt{WitnessArtifact}
record and verified against the formal model via \texttt{WitnessConsistentWithRuntime}.
When consistency holds, the formal elevation theorem applies within the Lean model.
