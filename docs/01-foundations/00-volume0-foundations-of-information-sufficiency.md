## 0. Master Epigraph & Core Thesis

> **Every decision requires information. Every information transformation incurs a cost to acquire, store, communicate, and compute. TAKT studies the minimal information necessary to preserve a relevant property and understands friction as the unavoidable cost of transporting that minimal information.**

### Foundational Thesis
Necessary friction is not a primitive parameter or an intrinsic defect. Necessary friction is a derived quantity: it is exactly the communication and computational cost required to transport the minimal sufficient information class $[f_P^*]$ for a target property $P$. Excess friction is simply the cost of transporting redundant information above $[f_P^*]$ in the Information Preorder lattice.

## 1. Domain-Agnostic Axioms of Information Transformations

Let $X$ be an arbitrary information domain (State Space, Trajectory Stream, Metric Space).

### Axiom 1.1 (Information Transformations)
An **information transformation** $f : X \to Z$ maps raw elements $x \in X$ into a compressed codomain $Z$.

### Axiom 1.2 (Information Preorder $\le_{\text{info}}$)
For any $f_1 : X \to Z_1$ and $f_2 : X \to Z_2$, $f_1$ is coarser than $f_2$ ($f_1 \le_{\text{info}} f_2$) iff there exists a factorizing map $h : Z_2 \to Z_1$ such that:
$$f_1 = h \circ f_2$$

### Axiom 1.3 (Information Equivalence $\sim_{\text{info}}$)
Two transformations $f_1, f_2$ carry isomorphic informative content ($f_1 \sim_{\text{info}} f_2$) iff:
$$f_1 \le_{\text{info}} f_2 \quad \land \quad f_2 \le_{\text{info}} f_1$$

The canonical objects of study in TAKT are the **equivalence classes $[f]_{\sim_{\text{info}}}$**.

---

## 2. Bounded Join-Semilattice Structure

The quotient space $\text{Trans}(X) / \sim_{\text{info}}$ under $\le_{\text{info}}$ forms a **Bounded Join-Semilattice**:

1. **Top Element ($\top = [\text{id}]$)**: Maximum information ($\text{id} : X \to X$). For all $f$, $f \le_{\text{info}} \text{id}$.
2. **Bottom Element ($\bot = [\text{const}_c]$)**: Minimum information ($\text{const}_c : X \to \{c\}$). For all $f$, $\text{const}_c \le_{\text{info}} f$.
3. **Join Operation ($\sqcup$)**: $[f_1] \sqcup [f_2] = [f_1 \times f_2]$ where $(f_1 \times f_2)(x) = (f_1(x), f_2(x))$.
   - **Commutativity**: $(f_1 \times f_2) \sim_{\text{info}} (f_2 \times f_1)$.
   - **Associativity**: $((f_1 \times f_2) \times f_3) \sim_{\text{info}} (f_1 \times (f_2 \times f_3))$.
   - **Least Upper Bound**: $f_1 \le_{\text{info}} g \land f_2 \le_{\text{info}} g \implies (f_1 \times f_2) \le_{\text{info}} g$.

## 3. Minimal Sufficiency & Principal Filter Theorem

Given a target property $P : X \to Y$:

### Definition 3.1 (Sufficiency)
Transformation $f : X \to Z$ is **sufficient for $P$** ($\text{IsSufficient } f P$) iff $\exists h : Z \to Y$ such that $P = h \circ f$.

### Definition 3.2 (Minimal Sufficiency)
$f_P^*$ is **minimal sufficient for $P$** iff $\text{IsSufficient } f_P^* P$ and for all sufficient $f$, $f_P^* \le_{\text{info}} f$.

### Theorem 3.3 (Uniqueness of Minimal Sufficiency Class)
Any two minimal sufficient transformations for $P$ belong to the same canonical equivalence class:
$$f_1^* \sim_{\text{info}} f_2^*$$

### Theorem 3.4 (Principal Upper Filter Characterization)
The set of all sufficient transformations for $P$ is precisely the **Principal Upper Filter $\uparrow [f_P^*]$** generated by $[f_P^*]$:
$$\text{Sufficient}(P) = \{ f : f_P^* \le_{\text{info}} f \} = \uparrow [f_P^*]$$

---

## 4. Universal Adjunction Equivalence

### Theorem 4.1 (Adjunction Equivalence)
For any minimal sufficient transformation $f_P^*$ associated with target property $P$:
$$f_P^* \le_{\text{info}} f \iff P \le_{\text{info}} f$$

### Theorem 4.2 (Property Refinement Monotonicity)
The minimal sufficiency mapping $\mathcal{M} : P \mapsto [f_P^*]$ is a monotone morphism from the preorder of target properties to the information semilattice:
$$P_1 \le_{\text{info}} P_2 \implies [f_{P_1}^*] \le_{\text{info}} [f_{P_2}^*]$$

## 5. Derived Necessary Friction (Research Program)

**Status:** *Foundational Research Program (Tesis & Programa de Investigación)*

To elevate Derived Friction from a foundational thesis to a fully proved theorem, three formal requirements must be established:

1. **Explicit Cost Model $C_{\text{trans}}(f)$**: Defining cost measure over communication complexity, bits, memory, or execution time.
2. **Infimum Attainment**: Proving $\inf_{f \in [f_P^*]} C_{\text{trans}}(f)$ is attained in discrete finite domains.
3. **Equivalence Class Cost Function**: Demonstrating representative-independence or defining cost directly over $[f]_{\sim_{\text{info}}}$.

### Definition 5.1 (Necessary Friction $\mathcal{F}^*(P)$)
The **Minimal Necessary Friction** required to satisfy target property $P$ is:
$$\mathcal{F}^*(P) = C_{\text{trans}}([f_P^*]) = \inf_{f \in [f_P^*]} C_{\text{trans}}(f)$$

### Definition 5.2 (Redundant Friction $\Delta\mathcal{F}(f, P)$)
For any operational transformation $f \in \uparrow [f_P^*]$, excess or redundant friction is:
$$\Delta\mathcal{F}(f, P) = C_{\text{trans}}(f) - \mathcal{F}^*(P) \ge 0$$

*Conclusion:* Friction is zero-redundant iff $f \in [f_P^*]$. All friction above $\mathcal{F}^*(P)$ stems strictly from transporting redundant information above $[f_P^*]$ in the Information Lattice.
