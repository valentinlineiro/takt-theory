# Structural Preservation Theory v1.1
## Canonical Core + Conservative Proxy Extension

**Status:** Integrated post-falsification. Core v1.0 intact. Proxy extension
added as a second layer when exact preservation is impossible.

---

## Part I: Canonical Core (v1.0, unchanged)

For the full core, see `docs/canonical-core-v1.0.md`. Summary:

**Axiom 1 (Pullback).** For every $f: X \to Y$ and $\sigma \in S_Y$,
$f^*(\sigma) \in S_X$ exists (pointwise for binary structures).

**Axiom 2 (Preorder).** $\preceq_Y$ is a preorder on $S_Y$ for every $Y$.

**Definition (Preservation).** $\mathcal{C}$ preserves $\Phi$ relative to
$(\sigma, \tau)$ when $\sigma_\mathcal{C} \preceq \tau_\Phi$.

**Theorem 4 (Equivalence repair).** Under equivalence structures,
refinement $(\mathcal{C}', \phi)$ restores preservation iff within each
$\mathcal{C}$-fibre, $\mathcal{C}'$ separates $\Phi$-distinct elements.

**Theorem 5 (Pseudometric boon).** Under pseudometric structures,
refinement preserves an already-holding preservation relation, but does
not repair a broken one without additional conditions.

---

## Part II: Refinement Asymmetry (Finding A)

| Theorem | Type | Power |
|---------|------|-------|
| 4 | Equivalence | **Repair**: refinement can fix broken preservation |
| 5 | Pseudometric | **Boon only**: refinement preserves existing preservation, cannot repair |

This is structural, not accidental: equivalence admits within-fibre
categorical separation; pseudometric requires within-fibre metric
dominance, which is a stronger condition.

When Theorem 4 fails (non-equivalence) or Theorem 5 cannot apply
(preservation already broken), a second mechanism is needed.

## Part III: Conservative Proxy Extension (v1.1)

### 1. Setting

Let $C: X \to Y$ be a morphism (any function).
Let $\Phi: X \to L$ be a property where $(L, \sqsubseteq)$ is a poset.

For each $y \in Y$, the fibre image is:

$$ S_y = \{\Phi(x) \mid C(x) = y\} \subseteq L $$

### 2. Existence condition

**Condition (Fibre meet).** For every $y \in Y$, the set $S_y$ has a
greatest lower bound (GLB, meet) in $(L, \sqsubseteq)$.

This is the **single condition** the extension requires. It holds for:
- $\mathbb{R}$ with $\leq$ (bounded-below subsets have infima)
- Powersets $\mathcal{P}(U)$ with $\subseteq$ (arbitrary intersections exist)
- Finite and complete lattices with $\sqsubseteq$
- Any poset after meet-preserving completion (Dedekind, ideal)

It fails for:
- $\mathbb{Q}$ with $\leq$ (e.g., $\{q \mid q^2 < 2\}$ has no GLB)
- Non-complete posets where fibre images lack GLBs

**Completion principle:** When the condition fails, extend $L$ to a
meet-preserving completion $\bar{L}$. After completion, the condition
holds and the proxy is defined. The completion never worsens the proxy.

### 3. The proxy

Define $\Phi^\downarrow: Y \to L$ by:

$$ \Phi^\downarrow(y) = \bigsqcap S_y = \bigsqcap_{x \in C^{-1}(y)} \Phi(x) $$

For empty fibres ($C^{-1}(y) = \varnothing$), define
$\Phi^\downarrow(y) = \top_L$ if it exists.

### 4. Core propositions

**Proposition 1 (Safety).** For all $x \in X$:
$\Phi^\downarrow(C(x)) \sqsubseteq \Phi(x)$.

*Follows from the GLB being a lower bound of $S_{C(x)}$.*

**Proposition 2 (Preservation).** $C$ preserves $\Phi^\downarrow$:
$C(x_1) = C(x_2) \implies \Phi^\downarrow(C(x_1)) = \Phi^\downarrow(C(x_2))$.

*Follows from $\Phi^\downarrow$ being defined on $Y$.*

**Proposition 3 (Optimality).** For any other safe proxy
$\Psi: Y \to L$ (satisfying $\Psi(C(x)) \sqsubseteq \Phi(x)$ for all $x$),
$\Psi(y) \sqsubseteq \Phi^\downarrow(y)$ for all $y$.

*Follows from the GLB being the GREATEST lower bound.*

**Proposition 4 (Compositionality).** If $C = C_2 \circ C_1$ and all
intermediate GLBs exist, then:

$$ \Phi^\downarrow_{C_2 \circ C_1} = (\Phi^\downarrow_{C_1})^\downarrow_{C_2} $$

*Follows from associativity of meets and the union structure of fibres
under composition.*

**Proposition 5 (Monotonicity under refinement).** If $C''$ refines $C'$
($C' = \phi \circ C''$), then:

$$ \Phi^\downarrow_{C'}(\phi(y'')) \sqsubseteq \Phi^\downarrow_{C''}(y'') $$

*A finer refinement gives a less conservative proxy. Refinement never
worsens the proxy.*

### 5. Usefulness (operational)

The proxy is **operationally useful** when $\Phi^\downarrow$ varies
across reachable fibres. It collapses to a constant ($\bot$ for all $y$)
when $\Phi$ varies orthogonally to $C$ — i.e., when $\Phi$ contains no
information recoverable from $C$.

**Collapse signals refinement need.** When the proxy is useless, the
core's refinement mechanism (Part I) applies: replace $C$ with $C'$
where $C = \phi \circ C'$ and $C'$ captures more of $\Phi$'s variation.

### Relationship between v1.0 and v1.1

| Aspect | v1.0 — Exact preservation | v1.1 — Conservative proxy |
|--------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Goal | Restore full preservation | Safe bound when restoration impossible |
| Mechanism | Refine C (add distinctions) | Meet-over-fibre of Φ |
| Structure type | Equivalence (Thm 4), Pseudometric (Thm 5) | Any meet-complete poset |
| Result | ~_C' ⊆ ~_Φ | Φ^↓ ≤ Φ pointwise |
| When it applies | Refinement feasible | Refinement infeasible or insufficient |
| Failure mode | — | Collapse → refine C (falls back to v1.0) |

## Part V: Open questions

1. **Proxy distance.** When the proxy exists, how far is it from the
   optimal refinement? Each domain has its own measure (Hoeffding bound,
   PSNR loss, certain answer gap). Is there a general distance notion?

2. **Threshold preservation.** For safety properties with a threshold
   $\theta$, the proxy gives $\Phi^\downarrow(y) \leq \theta \implies$
   "cannot guarantee safety." The relationship between proxy comparison
   to threshold and DECISION quality is not formalized.

3. **Dual proxy.** When does the upper envelope (join over fibre) give
   a useful optimistic bound? E.g., optimistic margin for risk-seeking
   decisions, possible worlds in databases.

4. **Non-poset codomains.** The extension assumes $(L, \sqsubseteq)$ is
   a poset (needed for GLB uniqueness). Can it be generalized to
   preorders (Axiom 2 of the core)? Yes — the GLB is unique up to
   equivalence.
